# The Lighthouse Bakery

A novel by Marta Lindqvist. Paperback, 288 pages, 40 short chapters.

When Freya inherits a bakery from an aunt she met only twice, she plans to sell it within the week. The bakery is in
an old lighthouse at the end of a fishing village, the oven is older than Freya, and the only staff member is a
seventeen-year-old called Tomas who can make perfect cinnamon buns and nothing else. Then the buyer pulls out, the
village's only cafe closes for the winter, and Freya finds her aunt's notebook of recipes, with notes in the margins
about every person in the village.

What follows is a year of early mornings, burnt loaves and small mysteries: who has been leaving fish on the
doorstep, why the harbour master orders bread he never collects, and what her aunt meant by "the Thursday order".
Freya slowly becomes part of a community she had planned to leave, and learns why her aunt chose to stay.

Lindqvist writes short, light chapters of six to eight pages, each built around one day in the bakery, which makes
it an easy book to pick up for twenty minutes on a train or a bus. There is a gentle romance, a lot of weather, and
a cast of village characters who are funny without being silly.

The novel is warm from start to finish. There are a few sad moments, mostly about the aunt, but the story ends with
the whole village at a midsummer party in the lighthouse, and readers describe the ending as joyful and hopeful.
It stands on its own; no other book is needed to follow it.
