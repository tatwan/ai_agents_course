# The Night Ferry

A thriller by Idris Calloway. Paperback, 412 pages, 31 chapters.

Nadia Farouk has worked the night shift on the ferry between two small island ports for eleven years. She knows every
regular passenger: the baker who sleeps in his van, the twins who sell lottery tickets, the old man who always asks
for tea with three sugars and never drinks it. So when a woman in a grey raincoat boards at midnight with no luggage
and no ticket, Nadia notices. By the time the ferry reaches open water, the woman has vanished, and the only trace of
her is a key wrapped in a page torn from a hotel register.

The crossing takes four hours. The radio is down because of the storm, the captain will not turn back, and one of
the forty passengers on board is lying about where they were when the woman disappeared. Nadia has until the lights
of the harbour appear to find out who.

Calloway tells the story hour by hour, moving between the passenger deck, the engine room and the captain's bridge,
with flashbacks to the hotel where the key came from. The chapters are long and end on a question, so most readers
will want to keep going well past bedtime. Reviewers have praised the closed setting and the slow build of suspicion
among people who thought they knew each other.

The ending is satisfying and fair: every clue is on the page, and the last chapter, back in the morning sunshine of
the harbour, ties up each passenger's story. Fans of classic locked-room mysteries will enjoy spotting the clues
early. At just over four hundred pages it is a long read, but a fast one.

Contains mild violence. Suitable for older teenagers and adults.
