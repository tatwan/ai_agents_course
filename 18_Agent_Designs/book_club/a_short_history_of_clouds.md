# A Short History of Clouds

Popular science by Dr. Samuel Osei. Paperback, 224 pages, 6 chapters.

Why does a cloud float when it weighs as much as a hundred elephants? Why do some clouds bring rain and others
never do, and how did a shy pharmacist in London give clouds the names we still use? Dr. Samuel Osei, a weather
scientist who has spent twenty years flying through storms in research aircraft, answers these questions and many
more in this short, curious book.

Osei moves from the science of water drops to the history of weather forecasting, from the first barometers to the
satellites and supercomputers of today. Along the way he tells the stories of the people who looked up and tried
to understand the sky: sailors, farmers, painters, and one balloonist who rose so high he almost lost
consciousness. The final chapter looks at what clouds can tell us about a changing climate, and ends on a hopeful
note about what better forecasts can do for farmers.

The book is short in pages but not a quick read. Each of its six chapters is around forty pages of dense
explanation, with diagrams that reward careful study. Readers who enjoy taking their time with an idea will love it;
readers who prefer to read in short bursts may find it hard to stop in the middle of a chapter and pick the thread up
again the next day.

Clear, enthusiastic and full of surprising facts, it has been praised by teachers and scientists alike. You will
never look at the sky the same way again.

Includes 30 black-and-white diagrams and a glossary.
